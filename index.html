A check is assigned to a shift by its time (07:00–15:00 = 1, 15:00–23:00 = 2, otherwise 3; checks before 07:00 count toward the previous day's third shift). "vs BOM" is the measured average against the BOM gas standard for the item(s) run that shift — positive means cans are getting more gas than the standard, negative less. "Corrected" counts heads where the operator wrote a correction. Individual readings aren't listed — use Export CSV for the full table.

Per-can figures = lb/case × 453.592 ÷ cans per case. "BOM split (mass)" = N₂O ÷ (N₂O + N₂) from the BOM quantities — if it matches the ratio column numerically, the BOM was written by mass while the blender runs by volume. Hover a code for its notes.

Conversions

scf to weight

What does the meter's number weigh?

The blender reports standard cubic feet. One scf of N₂O is 0.116 lb; one scf of N₂ is 0.0739 lb. Enter a quantity in any unit and the calculator gives the others — for a blend, it also splits the total between the two gases using the volume ratio the blender runs.

Fill weight to BOM

What should the BOM say?

Start from the gas that actually goes into a can (the gas weight check average), the case size, and the N₂O percentage set on the blender. The calculator works out the grams of each gas per can and the pounds per case to enter on the BOM lines. Choose whether the setpoint is meant by volume, which is how the blender meters it, or by mass.
